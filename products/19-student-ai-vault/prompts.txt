ESSAY PLANNING (20 Prompts)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[EW-01] Essay outline generator:
"I need to write a [word count] word [essay type: argumentative/analytical/persuasive/expository] essay on the topic: '[topic]'. My thesis is: '[thesis statement or leave blank for suggestions]. Create a detailed outline with: introduction structure, [3-5] body paragraphs each with a topic sentence and 2-3 supporting points, and conclusion approach. My class is [subject], level: [high school/undergrad/postgrad]."

[EW-02] Thesis statement builder:
"I'm writing an essay on [topic] for [class/level]. Here are my initial thoughts: [your ideas]. Help me craft 3 different thesis statements ranging from: safe/straightforward → more argumentative → most analytical/complex. For each thesis, briefly explain the essay structure it implies."

[EW-03] Argument strengthener:
"I'm arguing that [your position] in my essay about [topic]. Generate the 5 strongest counterarguments someone could make against my position. Then for each counterargument, show me how to rebut it or incorporate it into my argument to make it stronger."

[EW-04] Evidence finder prompt:
"I'm writing about [topic] and need evidence to support this claim: '[your claim]'. Suggest 5 types of evidence I should look for: statistics, academic studies, historical examples, expert quotes, and real-world case studies. Tell me what to search for specifically."

[EW-05] Introduction writer:
"Write 3 different introductions for an essay about [topic] with thesis: '[thesis]'. Style 1: start with a surprising statistic. Style 2: start with a relevant anecdote or scenario. Style 3: start with a provocative question. Each intro: 100-150 words, ending with my thesis."

[EW-06] Body paragraph structure:
"Write a model body paragraph for my essay about [topic] that argues: '[point]. Include: topic sentence, 2 pieces of evidence with analysis, a quote placeholder, and a transition sentence. Length: 150-200 words. Academic writing style."

[EW-07] Conclusion writer:
"Write a strong conclusion for my essay on [topic] with thesis: '[thesis]'. My main arguments were: [1], [2], [3]. The conclusion should: restate thesis in new words, synthesize (not summarize) my arguments, end with a broader implication or call to thought. 150-200 words."

[EW-08] Essay feedback prompt:
"Review this essay draft for: argument strength, evidence quality, structure clarity, transitions, and academic voice. Be specific — cite exact quotes from my writing. Prioritize: [what I want feedback on most]. Here's my essay: [paste essay]"

[EW-09] Paraphrase without plagiarism:
"Help me understand this quote/passage: '[paste text]'. First, explain what it means in simple terms. Then show me 3 ways to incorporate this idea into my own writing using paraphrase — changing structure and vocabulary while keeping the meaning. Avoid plagiarism."

[EW-10] Academic voice improver:
"Rewrite these sentences in appropriate academic language without losing the meaning. Make them: more formal, more precise, and better supported. Original: [paste your informal sentences]. Keep them my sentences — just improve the register."

[EW-11] Citation help (format fixer): "I need to cite these sources in [MLA/APA/Chicago/Harvard] format: [source details]. Create properly formatted in-text citations AND bibliography/reference list entries for each."

[EW-12] Topic sentence generator: "I'm writing body paragraphs about: [list your body paragraph topics]. Write a strong topic sentence for each that: states the argument clearly, connects to my thesis '[thesis]', and sets up the evidence I'll present."

[EW-13] Transition sentence bank: "My essay transitions from [paragraph A topic] to [paragraph B topic]. Write 5 different transition sentences I could use. Vary: connective words, structure, and tone."

[EW-14] Word count expander: "My paragraph is [X words] but needs to be [Y words]. Here is the paragraph: [paste]. Expand it by: adding more specific evidence, deepening the analysis, and addressing a counterargument. Don't add fluff — add substance."

[EW-15] Word count reducer: "My essay is [X words] over limit. Here is the essay: [paste]. Cut [X words] while keeping all key arguments. Prioritize: removing redundancy, tightening language, and cutting weakest evidence."

[EW-16] Compare/contrast essay framework: "I'm writing a compare/contrast essay on [Topic A] vs [Topic B] for [class]. Should I use block structure or point-by-point? Create an outline for whichever is stronger for this specific comparison. Include: [number] comparison criteria with specific points for each."

[EW-17] Persuasive essay techniques: "My persuasive essay topic is [topic]. Show me how to use these 5 rhetorical techniques in my essay: ethos, pathos, logos, anaphora, and rhetorical questions. Give me one specific sentence example of each I can adapt."

[EW-18] Research paper introduction: "Write an introduction for my research paper on [topic]. Include: hook (surprising fact about the topic), background context (3-4 sentences), gap in current research (what hasn't been studied), and research question/thesis. Length: 200-250 words. Academic register."

[EW-19] Abstract writer: "Write an abstract for my research paper. My research question: [question]. My methodology: [brief description]. My main findings: [findings]. My conclusion: [conclusion]. Abstract should be 150-250 words, past tense, no citations, all key elements included."

[EW-20] Literature review structure: "I'm writing a literature review on [topic] for [academic level]. I have [X] sources. Show me how to organize them thematically (not just source by source). Suggested themes to group around: [themes if known, or ask AI to suggest]."

EDITING & PROOFREADING (20 Prompts)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[EW-21] Grammar check: "Proofread this text for grammar errors, spelling mistakes, and punctuation issues. List each error, explain why it's wrong, and provide the correction: [paste text]"
[EW-22] Clarity checker: "Which sentences in this paragraph are unclear or confusing? Rewrite them for clarity: [paste paragraph]"
[EW-23] Passive voice finder: "Identify all passive voice constructions in this text and rewrite them in active voice where appropriate: [paste text]"
[EW-24] Repetition finder: "Find overused words and repetitive phrases in this text. Suggest alternatives: [paste text]"
[EW-25] Flow checker: "Does this essay flow logically from paragraph to paragraph? Where are the transitions weak? What's missing or abrupt? [paste essay]"
[EW-26] Consistency checker: "Check this essay for: consistent tense, consistent point of view, consistent citation style, consistent argument throughout: [paste essay]"
[EW-27] Argument checker: "Does my argument in this essay hold together logically? Are there any logical fallacies, unsupported claims, or contradictions? [paste essay]"
[EW-28] Academic tone checker: "Is the language in this essay appropriate for [academic level]? Flag any informal language, colloquialisms, or vague terms: [paste text]"
[EW-29] Plagiarism awareness: "I paraphrased this source passage. Is my paraphrase different enough to avoid plagiarism? Source: [original]. My version: [your version]."

STUDY & REVISION PROMPTS (30)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[EP-01] Topic summary generator:
"Summarize [topic] for a [grade/level] student who has [X days] until the exam. Include: key concepts, important definitions (5-10), the most commonly tested points, and 3 things that are easy to mix up. Use simple language — I need to understand this, not just memorize it."

[EP-02] Concept explainer:
"Explain [concept] in the simplest possible way. Use: one real-world analogy, one worked example, and a 3-sentence summary I could use as a definition. I'm a [level] student in [subject]."

[EP-03] Flashcard generator:
"Create [number] flashcards for [topic] in [subject]. Format: FRONT: [question or term] | BACK: [answer or definition]. Include: key terms, important dates/numbers, cause-effect relationships, and commonly confused concepts."

[EP-04] Practice test creator:
"Create a [number]-question practice test for [topic] in [subject]. Include: [X] multiple choice, [X] short answer, [X] long answer. Similar to what I'd see on [exam type: SAT/AP/university midterm/GCSE]. Include answer key with explanations."

[EP-05] Spaced repetition schedule:
"I have [X weeks] until my exam on [subject]. Topics to cover: [list]. Create a day-by-day spaced repetition study schedule that: covers each topic multiple times, increases difficulty over time, includes active recall sessions. [X hours] available per day."

[EP-06] Mnemonics creator:
"Create memorable mnemonics or memory tricks for: [list of things to memorize — dates, formulas, sequences, etc.]. Make them: catchy, logical, or funny (whatever works better for memory). Test: would a stressed student remember these during an exam?"

[EP-07] "Explain like I'm 5" then escalate:
"First explain [concept] in the simplest possible terms (like I'm 10). Then explain it at [my level]. Then tell me the one nuance that separates an A answer from a B answer on this topic."

[EP-08] Common mistakes guide:
"What are the 5 most common mistakes students make on exams about [topic]? For each: what the mistake is, why students make it, and how to avoid it."

[EP-09] Past paper question patterns:
"I'm preparing for [exam name/type] on [subject]. Based on typical exam patterns, what types of questions appear most often on [topic]? What makes an answer score full marks vs. partial marks?"

[EP-10] Mind map prompt:
"Create a text-based mind map of [topic] showing: the main concept at center, 4-6 major branches, sub-branches under each, and how concepts connect to each other. I'll use this as a revision overview."

[EP-11] Quick revision sheet: "Create a 1-page revision cheat sheet for [topic]. Maximum information in minimum space: key facts, formulas, definitions, dates, important quotes."
[EP-12] Essay plan under timed conditions: "Give me a 10-minute timed essay plan for the question: '[exam question]'. Structure: thesis (1 min), evidence per paragraph (6 min), conclusion (3 min). Go."
[EP-13] Examiner's perspective: "How would an examiner at [level] mark this response to the question '[question]': [my answer]. What marks would it get and why?"
[EP-14] Difficult concept breakdown: "I can't understand [concept]. I've tried [what you've tried]. Explain it from a completely different angle — use [metaphor/diagram description/story]."
[EP-15] Prediction questions: "Based on [topic] in [subject], generate 10 likely exam questions for [exam type]. Include mark allocations and what a full-mark answer must include."

SUBJECT-SPECIFIC STUDY PROMPTS (50)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

MATHEMATICS:
[SM-01] "Explain step-by-step how to solve this type of problem: [problem type]. Show all working. Then give me 3 practice problems of increasing difficulty."
[SM-02] "I keep making mistakes on [type of calculation]. Show me the most common errors and how to avoid each one."
[SM-03] "Create a formula sheet for [math topic] with: formula name, the formula, when to use it, worked example."

SCIENCES:
[SS-01] "Explain the mechanism behind [biological/chemical/physical process] using the analogy of [everyday thing]. Then explain it at exam level."
[SS-02] "Create a comparison table for [2-3 similar concepts in science]: [concept 1], [concept 2]. Include: definition, key features, examples, common exam questions."
[SS-03] "What experiment would I design to test [hypothesis]? Include: variables, controls, method, expected results, error sources."

HISTORY/SOCIAL SCIENCES:
[SH-01] "Analyze [historical event] using [PEEL/OREO/argument framework]. What are the strongest arguments for and against [historical interpretation]?"
[SH-02] "Create a timeline of key events for [period/topic] with: date, event, significance (1-2 sentences each), connection to [broader theme]."
[SH-03] "What are the [causes/consequences] of [historical event]? Rank them by importance and explain how a historian would evaluate each."

LITERATURE/ENGLISH:
[SL-01] "Analyze the use of [literary device] in [text/poem/passage]. Quote 3 examples, explain each one, and connect to [theme/author's purpose]."
[SL-02] "Compare [Character A] and [Character B] in [text] in terms of: motivation, development, relationship to theme. Structured as exam paragraph format."
[SL-03] "What are the key themes in [text] and how does the author develop each? Include specific quotes I can use in my essay."

BUSINESS/ECONOMICS:
[SB-01] "Explain [economic concept] using a real-world example from [industry/country/scenario]. Include: definition, diagram description, analysis, evaluation."
[SB-02] "Apply [business model/framework] to [company]. What are the implications for [business decision/strategy]?"
[SB-03] "Evaluate [economic policy] — what are the benefits, costs, and who are the stakeholders affected? Exam-style evaluative conclusion."

COMPUTER SCIENCE:
[SC-01] "Explain [algorithm] with: pseudocode, trace table example, time complexity, and when to use vs. not use it."
[SC-02] "Debug this code and explain what was wrong: [paste code]. What would the output be after fixing?"
[SC-03] "Explain [programming concept] using a real-world analogy, then show a code example."

[RP-01] Research question generator: "My broad topic is [topic] for [class/level]. Help me narrow it to a specific, researchable question. It should be: not too broad, not too narrow, original enough to be interesting, achievable with [X weeks] and [resource availability]."

[RP-02] Source evaluation: "Evaluate this source for academic credibility: [author/publication/URL]. Assess: authority (who wrote it?), accuracy (is it evidence-based?), purpose (why was it published?), currency (when?), relevance (to my topic). Should I cite it?"

[RP-03] Research plan: "Create a research plan for my project on [topic]. Timeline: [X weeks]. Include: research questions (primary and secondary), source types to find, data collection method, analysis approach, writing schedule."

[RP-04] Primary vs secondary sources: "My research topic is [topic]. Suggest: 3 types of primary sources I could find or collect, 5 reliable secondary source types to search. Include specific databases, journals, or search terms to use."

[RP-05] Survey/questionnaire creator: "I'm doing primary research on [topic] for [class/level]. Create a [number]-question survey that: measures [what you're trying to find out], uses appropriate question types (Likert/multiple choice/open), avoids bias, and can be completed in under [X minutes]."

[RP-06] Data analysis guide: "I've collected this data: [describe or paste data]. Help me: identify patterns, suggest appropriate analysis methods, explain what the findings might mean for my research question."

[RP-07] Group project coordinator: "I'm in a group project on [topic] with [X people] and [Y weeks]. Create: task breakdown by person, timeline with milestones, communication plan, quality checkpoints. How should we divide this specific project?"

[RP-08] Presentation script: "Create a [X minute] presentation script on [topic] for [audience: class/professor/competition]. Include: opening hook, key points with transitions, speaker notes, Q&A prep questions. Slides structure: [number of slides]."

[RP-09] Science lab report: "Write a model lab report structure for an experiment on [topic]. Include: title, abstract format, introduction requirements, method format, results presentation, discussion framework, conclusion checklist."

[RP-10] Case study analysis: "Analyze this case study: [paste or describe]. Apply [framework: SWOT/Porter's/PEST/other] to identify: key issues, stakeholder impacts, possible solutions, recommended action with justification."
