BOOK CONCEPT TEMPLATE:
[BC-01] Working title:
[BC-02] The promise (what reader gets): By reading this book, [reader] will [specific outcome].
[BC-03] Target reader: [Specific person at specific moment: "35-year-old professional who..."]
[BC-04] Core premise in one sentence: This book argues that [main thesis/idea].
[BC-05] Why you're the right author: [Your credibility + lived experience]
[BC-06] Why now: [Market timing, relevance, gap this fills]
[BC-07] Competing books and how yours differs: [2-3 comps + your differentiation]
[BC-08] Approximate length: [Business/self-help: 40-70k words | Memoir: 70-90k | Fiction: 80-100k+]

CHAPTER OUTLINE TEMPLATES (5 book types):
[CO-01] NON-FICTION/BUSINESS CHAPTER TEMPLATE:
Chapter [X]: [Working title]
One-sentence chapter premise: [What this chapter argues or teaches]
Chapter goal: By end of chapter, reader will [know/be able to/understand]
Key points (3-5): [List main content points]
Stories/examples to include: [Specific case studies, research, personal stories]
How it connects to next chapter: [Transition logic]
Approximate word count: [X,000 words]

[CO-02] MEMOIR CHAPTER TEMPLATE:
Chapter [X]: [Title or scene title]
Timeline: [When this happens in the story]
Scene: [What happens — the external event]
Internal experience: [How you felt, what you thought, what changed in you]
Theme served: [The larger meaning this scene contributes to]
What reader learns or feels: [Intended reader experience]

[CO-03] SELF-HELP CHAPTER TEMPLATE:
Problem introduced | Concept taught | Evidence presented | Exercise or reflection | Takeaway

[CO-04] NOVEL CHAPTER TEMPLATE:
POV character | Scene goal | Conflict | Turning point | Ends with: hook into next chapter

[CO-05] ESSAY COLLECTION TEMPLATE:
Essay [X] title | Central argument | Opening hook | Supporting sections | Closing resonance

FULL BOOK OUTLINE FORMATS (5 non-fiction structures):
[FO-01] Problem-solution arc: Chapters 1-3 establish problem → chapters 4-8 teach solution → final chapter shows transformation
[FO-02] Before-after-transformation: Author's before state → journey → what changed → how reader can do same
[FO-03] Framework book: Introduce your [3/5/7]-step framework → one chapter per step → integration chapter
[FO-04] Research-driven: What the research says + author synthesis + practical application
[FO-05] Story-driven: Lead with narrative throughout → weave in teaching → readers learn through story

SECTION 2: CHATGPT WRITING ASSISTANCE (80 Templates)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[WA-01] Chapter outline: "Create a detailed outline for Chapter [X] of my book. Chapter premise: [premise]. Key points to cover: [list]. My voice/tone: [description]. Target reader: [description]. Chapter length target: [X words]."

[WA-02] Opening paragraph: "Write 3 different opening paragraphs for Chapter [X] about [topic]. Opening styles: 1) Start in scene 2) Start with a provocative statement 3) Start with a surprising statistic. My voice: [description]."

[WA-03] Research synthesis: "I have these research findings relevant to Chapter [X]: [list or paste]. Synthesize them into a cohesive narrative that supports my argument: [argument]. Make it accessible to [reader level]."

[WA-04] Anecdote development: "Help me develop this brief anecdote into a full scene: [your notes]. Add: sensory details, emotional texture, dialogue if appropriate. My writing voice: [description]."

[WA-05] Transition writing: "Write 3 different transition sentences/paragraphs to connect these two sections: [end of section 1] → [beginning of section 2]. The connection should feel [natural / surprising / logical]."

[WA-06] First draft chapter: "Write a [X word] draft of a chapter for my [book type] book about [topic]. Chapter premise: [one sentence]. Key points to cover: [list]. Tone: [description]. Assume the reader knows: [what they know]. Do NOT assume they know: [what they don't]."

[WA-07] Edit for voice consistency: "Read this passage: [paste]. Then rewrite it to match the voice in this sample passage from the same book: [paste sample]. Keep the content but adjust: sentence length, word choice, rhythm, formality."

[WA-08] Feedback and critique: "Act as a developmental editor. Read this chapter: [paste]. Evaluate: 1) Is the premise clear? 2) Does it deliver on its promise? 3) Are there pacing issues? 4) What's missing? 5) What could be cut? Be specific and direct."

[WA-09 to WA-80] More: chapter conclusion templates, exercises/reflection questions, bibliography research, book title options, subtitle options, author bio, book description, back cover copy, table of contents organization, foreword draft, acknowledgments

SECTION 3: EDITING & REFINING (80 Templates)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DEVELOPMENTAL EDITING CHECKLIST:
□ Does every chapter deliver on what the title/heading promises?
□ Is the overall arc clear? (Where are we going and why?)
□ Are there any chapters that could be cut without losing the book?
□ Are stories/examples directly relevant or just interesting?
□ Does the opening chapter hook the reader and establish the promise?
□ Does the final chapter deliver a satisfying conclusion?
□ Is the voice consistent throughout?
□ Are there any logical gaps in the argument?

LINE EDITING SELF-REVIEW (10 prompts):
[LE-01] Read aloud test: Anything you stumble over needs rewriting
[LE-02] Sentence variety: Varied length? Not all long, not all short?
[LE-03] Passive voice: Find "was/were + verb" constructions → rewrite active
[LE-04] Adverb pruning: "walked quickly" → "strode" | "said loudly" → "shouted"
[LE-05] Show vs tell: "She was nervous" → describe what nervous looks like
[LE-06] Clichés: Flag and replace each one
[LE-07] Repetition: Same word twice in one paragraph?
[LE-08] Vague words: "things," "stuff," "nice," "very" → be specific
[LE-09] Overexplaining: If you said it, don't repeat it different words
[LE-10] Jargon: If reader might not know this word, replace or define

BETA READER FEEDBACK TEMPLATE:
Questions to give beta readers:
1. What was the most valuable chapter/section for you?
2. Was there any section that felt slow or where you wanted to stop reading?
3. Was the core argument/message clear throughout?
4. Is there a question you had that the book didn't answer?
5. Who else should read this book?
6. Would you recommend it? Why / why not?

TRADITIONAL VS SELF-PUBLISHING COMPARISON:
Traditional: 1-3 year timeline | 8-15% royalty | Free distribution/marketing support | Prestige | Requires agent/query
Self-publishing (KDP): 60-90 day timeline | 35-70% royalty | You control everything | Requires your own marketing

QUERY LETTER TEMPLATE (traditional):
"Dear [Agent name],

[HOOK — 1 sentence that makes them want to read more]

[BOOK DESCRIPTION — 2-3 paragraphs: what it's about, who the protagonist is, what's at stake, what makes it unique]

[MARKET/COMP TITLES — "Readers of [Comp Book A] and [Comp Book B] will enjoy this book because...]

[ABOUT YOU — Brief: relevant credentials, platform, why you wrote this book]

[CLOSING — "I'd be happy to send the full manuscript. Thank you for your time."]

Sincerely, [Name]"
