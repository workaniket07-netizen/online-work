IN-THE-MOMENT GROUNDING EXERCISES (20):

[AT-01] THE 5-4-3-2-1 TECHNIQUE (most evidence-based):
When anxiety spikes, bring yourself back to the present:
5 things you can SEE: [Look around and name them specifically]
4 things you can TOUCH: [Feel them physically — texture, temperature]
3 things you can HEAR: [Name each sound]
2 things you can SMELL: [Notice what's in the air]
1 thing you can TASTE: [What's in your mouth right now?]
Time: 2-3 minutes | Evidence: Interrupts the anxiety loop by activating present-moment sensory awareness.

[AT-02] BOX BREATHING (tactical breathing):
Inhale slowly for 4 counts → Hold for 4 counts → Exhale for 4 counts → Hold for 4 counts → Repeat 4-6 times
Works because: Activates the parasympathetic nervous system, directly counteracting the stress response.
Use when: Panic spike, pre-presentation, post-argument, before difficult conversation.

[AT-03] THE CALM PLACE VISUALIZATION:
Close your eyes. Breathe deeply.
Visualize a place that feels completely safe and peaceful. This can be real or imagined.
Notice: What do you see? What sounds are there? What does the air feel like? What scents?
Stay for 2-5 minutes. Anchor this place — you can return here anytime.

[AT-04] PROGRESSIVE MUSCLE RELAXATION (PMR):
Starting with your feet: Tense all muscles for 5 seconds → Release completely → Notice the difference.
Move upward: feet → calves → thighs → stomach → hands → arms → shoulders → face.
Full practice: 15-20 minutes. Short version: 5-7 minutes (focus on shoulders/jaw area where tension lives).

[AT-05] THE SAFE CONTAINER EXERCISE (for intrusive thoughts):
Visualize a container (box, safe, chest) that feels completely secure.
When an anxious thought comes, imagine placing it in the container. Close the lid.
Tell yourself: "I can open this later when I have more resources. For now, it's safe there."
This is NOT avoidance — it's buying yourself regulatory time.

[AT-06] COLD WATER DIVE (for intense anxiety):
Splash cold water on your face or hold ice cubes.
This activates the dive reflex, slowing heart rate rapidly.
Works in 30-60 seconds for acute anxiety.

[AT-07] PHYSIOLOGICAL SIGH:
Double inhale through the nose (sniff twice to fully inflate lungs) → Long slow exhale through the mouth.
Proven to deflate air sacs and reset CO2/O2 balance = fastest breath-based anxiety relief.
Do 1-3 times.

[AT-08 to AT-20] More grounding: counting backward by 7, category naming game, physical exercise burst (burns off cortisol), tapping (EFT), humming or singing, cold shower, walking barefoot, smell something strong, petting an animal

COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING EXERCISES (20):
[CR-01] THE THOUGHT LOG (CBT core tool):
Situation: [What happened?]
Automatic thought: [What your mind said immediately]
Emotion(s): [What you felt] Rate intensity: /10
Evidence FOR the thought:
Evidence AGAINST the thought:
Balanced alternative thought:
New emotion intensity: /10

[CR-02] THE DECATASTROPHIZING EXERCISE:
My anxious prediction: [What I fear will happen]
Worst possible outcome: [Rate probability 0-100%]
Best possible outcome: [Rate probability 0-100%]
Most realistic outcome: [Rate probability 0-100%]
If worst happens, could I cope? [How?]
What I'd tell a friend in this situation:

[CR-03] THE "WOULD A WISE FRIEND SAY THAT?" EXERCISE:
Write out your anxious inner voice statement.
Now write what a wise, caring friend would say in response.
Which one is more useful? More accurate? More kind?

[CR-04 to CR-20] More cognitive tools: examining evidence, double standard technique, cost-benefit of worrying, "is this helpful?" question, distancing (call yourself by name), future self perspective, probability calibration

WORRY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (20):
[WM-01] THE WORRY WINDOW: Set one 15-minute "worry time" per day. When worries arise outside that window, note them down and postpone them. During worry window: address each worry fully.
[WM-02] PRODUCTIVE VS UNPRODUCTIVE WORRY: Is this worry about something actionable? → Problem-solve. Not actionable? → Acceptance practice.
[WM-03] THE WHAT-IF LADDER: Trace your worry to its end: "What if [X] happens? → Then [Y] → Then [Z] → Ultimately: [root fear]." Name the root fear. Address that directly.
[WM-04 to WM-20] More worry tools: uncertainty tolerance building, scheduling reassurance, distraction strategies (when useful), sleep and anxiety, "not my thoughts" practice

BEHAVIORAL ACTIVATION (core CBT/depression tool):
[BA-01] Activity scheduling: Depression reduces motivation. Schedule activities anyway — behavior precedes motivation. Start with small, easy activities that gave you pleasure before.
[BA-02] The mastery/pleasure tracker: Rate each daily activity for: Mastery (how accomplished you felt, 0-10) and Pleasure (how good it felt, 0-10). Identify patterns.
[BA-03] Opposite action: When the depressed voice says "don't do anything," do the opposite — small version.
[BA-04] Energy forecast: On lower-energy days, identify your 1 minimum viable action.

SELF-COMPASSION PRACTICES (20):
[SC-01] THE SELF-COMPASSION BREAK (Kristin Neff):
In a moment of pain, place your hand on your heart and say:
"This is a moment of suffering." (mindfulness)
"Suffering is part of being human." (common humanity)
"May I be kind to myself in this moment." (self-kindness)
Custom version: "May I [give myself what I need right now]."

[SC-02] THE FRIEND TEST: How would you talk to your best friend going through what you're going through? Write that response. Direct it at yourself.

[SC-03] ORIGIN OF THE INNER CRITIC: Where does the critical voice come from? Whose voice does it sound like? Does this voice have permission to speak to you this way?

[SC-04 to SC-20] More self-compassion: loving-kindness meditation, self-criticism vs. accountability distinction, perfectionism and depression connection, when standards become self-harm, rewriting your inner narrative

BOUNDARY-SETTING SCRIPTS (30):
[BS-01] The basic no: "I'm not going to be able to do that." [No explanation needed. Practice saying this without JADE: Justify, Argue, Defend, Explain]

[BS-02] Declining with warmth: "I appreciate you thinking of me. I can't commit to that right now."

[BS-03] Saying no to family (harder): "I love you and I'm not going to be able to do that. I hope we can still [specific connection]."

[BS-04] Saying no to a boss: "I want to make sure I deliver quality on my current priorities. Taking this on right now would compromise that. Can we talk about what to deprioritize?"

[BS-05] Response to guilt-tripping: "I understand you're disappointed. My answer is still no."

[BS-06] Ending overstaying conversations: "I need to wrap this up — I have [other commitment]. Let's continue another time."

[BS-07] Setting limits with chronic oversharing: "I care about you and I've noticed this topic comes up a lot. I want to support you, and I'm also noticing I'm not the best support for this. Have you considered talking to [therapist/resource]?"

[BS-08] The broken record technique: Repeat your limit calmly without argument: "[Limit], [Limit], [Limit]."

[BS-09 to BS-30] More boundary scripts: online/digital limits, emotional dumping, unsolicited advice, boundary violations response, re-setting violated limits, when someone doesn't respect your limits

[SE-02] STRENGTHS INVENTORY: List 20 genuine strengths — include character traits, skills, experiences overcome, values you hold.
[SE-03] VALUES CLARIFICATION: What matters most to me? Am I living in alignment with those values? Where are the gaps?
[SE-04 to SE-25] More: comparison detox, achievements timeline, compliment collection, inner child work, reframing past experiences, identity outside roles
